Decentralized Social Network

MILAN is a privacy-first decentralized social network designed around a simple idea: your digital identity and data should not have to belong to a centralized social platform.

Instead of treating the platform as the permanent home for your social life, MILAN is built around decentralized identity (DID), Decentralized Web Node (DWN) architecture, privacy and user-controlled sharing.

What makes MILAN decentralized?

DID identity

A decentralized identifier provides an identity layer designed to be less dependent on a single centralized account system.

DWN data layer

Decentralized Web Node architecture is used as the user-centered data layer for social records, media and permissions.

Permission-first sharing

Privacy and sharing are treated as product controls so users can decide what stays private and what they share.

Decentralized social network vs. traditional social media

Traditional social networks generally centralize identity, storage and the social graph inside one company's infrastructure. A decentralized model aims to separate those concerns and give people more control over identity, data and permissions.

MILAN combines social experiences with a Web5-oriented architecture using DID and DWN concepts. Its goal is not simply to move an existing feed onto a different backend, but to make user ownership and privacy part of the underlying model.

Frequently asked questions

How does a decentralized social network work?

A decentralized social network separates identity, data and permissions from a single centralized platform. In the MILAN model, decentralized identity (DID) provides the identity layer while a Decentralized Web Node (DWN) provides a user-centered data layer for records, media and permissions.

Benefits of a decentralized social network

The main benefit is greater control over identity and data. A decentralized approach can also make permissions more explicit, reduce dependence on one platform's infrastructure and give users a clearer relationship with their own digital records.

Examples and use cases

Decentralized social networks can support communities, creators, private sharing and social applications where identity and data are designed to remain user-controlled. MILAN applies these ideas to a privacy-first social experience built around DID and DWN.

Privacy and alternatives to centralized social media

A decentralized social network can be a privacy-focused alternative to traditional platforms when identity, data storage and sharing permissions are not controlled entirely by one company. MILAN combines these principles with social features while keeping user control at the center of the architecture.

What is a decentralized social network?

A decentralized social network is a social platform designed so identity, data, permissions or other core components are not controlled entirely by one central service. Different projects implement decentralization in different ways.

Is MILAN a decentralized social network?

MILAN positions itself as a privacy-first decentralized social network built around DID and DWN architecture, with a focus on user-owned data and permission-based sharing.

What is a DWN?

DWN stands for Decentralized Web Node. In the MILAN architecture, it is the user-centered data layer for storing and accessing digital records.

What is DID?

DID stands for Decentralized Identifier. It is a decentralized identity standard designed to let an entity identify itself without relying on a traditional centralized identity provider.

How is MILAN related to Web5?

MILAN uses Web5 concepts including decentralized identity and DWN architecture as part of its model for user-owned data and privacy-first social experiences.
