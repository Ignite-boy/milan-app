Five principles behind MILAN.

The experience can include communication, media, discovery and future digital services, but the foundation stays consistent: people first, ownership first, permission first.

01 · IDENTITY

Your identity belongs with you.

MILAN is built around decentralized identity concepts so your digital presence can be anchored to you rather than to a single platform account.

02 · DATA

Your data is an asset you control.

DWN architecture gives the ecosystem a path toward user-controlled data storage and portability instead of making one company the permanent gatekeeper.

03 · PRIVACY

Share by permission.

Private, public and person-to-person sharing should be deliberate choices—not the invisible default of an advertising system.

04 · VALUE

Value should flow back to people.

MILAN's long-term vision is to explore ways people can participate in economic value created from their digital presence, with clear consent and transparent rules.

05 · ECOSYSTEM

One ecosystem. Many possibilities.

Connection is only one layer. Web5 infrastructure can support identity, data, communication, media, AI-assisted experiences and future digital services in one user-owned ecosystem.

Why the Web5 idea matters.

Web2 made the internet incredibly useful, but many digital services also concentrated identity, data and economic power inside platforms. Web5 ideas point toward a different model: decentralized identity, user-controlled data and applications that can work with a person's digital life without requiring permanent ownership of it.

MILAN's ambition is to turn that direction into a practical ecosystem—simple enough for everyday people, powerful enough for builders, and designed around ownership from the start.

Frequently asked questions.

Is MILAN a social media app?

MILAN may include social and communication experiences, but its broader product direction is a Web5 ecosystem centered on identity, data ownership, privacy and user-controlled digital relationships.

What does Web5 mean in the MILAN vision?

In the MILAN context, Web5 represents a user-centered approach to digital identity and data: your identity and data should be portable, controllable and useful across experiences rather than permanently locked inside one platform.

What are DID and DWN?

A DID is a decentralized identifier concept used to represent a digital identity. A DWN is a decentralized web node concept for storing and accessing data under user-controlled rules.

Will users be able to earn from their data?

That is part of MILAN's long-term vision, but specific earning mechanisms depend on the product and economic models that are actually implemented. The principle is consent, transparency and user participation—not selling data secretly.
